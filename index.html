Treatment options

Treat only after you've found a live louse. Over-the-counter products are the usual first try. Resistance to them has been reported, so if live lice are still there after a full round and retreatment, ask a doctor about a prescription option. Ask your pharmacist what is available now, since products and rules change.

Use products exactly as labeled.They are insecticides. Don't use extra, and don't combine different lice products unless a provider or pharmacist tells you to. Rinse over a sink with warm water, not in the bath, and keep products out of eyes.
Want help from a professional?Lice removal clinics and in-home services exist in many areas. Search "lice removal near me," ask your school nurse or pediatrician, and check reviews and prices first.
Not working?If live lice remain after a full course and retreatment, don't keep repeating the same product. Ask your doctor or pharmacist about a different one.

How to check, step by step

Plan on 10 to 20 minutes per head. Longer or thicker hair takes longer.

  1. Sit your child in bright light. Wet the hair and add plain conditioner so lice move slowly.
  2. Comb with a fine-toothed lice comb from the scalp to the end of the hair, one small section at a time.
  3. Wipe the comb on a white paper towel after every pass and look for crawling bugs or tiny tan specks.
  4. Check behind the ears and at the back of the neck first. These are the warmest spots.
  5. Do the whole head, then wash the comb in hot water.

Myths and facts

Lice mean a dirty child or home.Lice don't care about cleanliness. They spread by head-to-head contact.
Lice jump or fly.They crawl only. Sharing hats and brushes is a much smaller risk than close contact.
The whole house needs deep cleaning.Lice live only a day or so off the head. Wash recent bedding and hats, and skip the rest.
Kids must stay home until every nit is gone.Pediatric guidance discourages "no-nit" rules. Most kids can go back to school after treatment starts.
